Personal Health Record Exporter

An open-source tool that lets a patient download their own medical record from their healthcare provider's patient portal, onto their own computer.

Read-only Runs locally No server, no account MIT licensed

This app connects to a healthcare organization's standards-based patient API, downloads the signed-in patient's own record, and turns it into a readable document — clinical notes, lab results, medications, problems, allergies, immunizations, and visit history — alongside the underlying structured data in FHIR® format.

It runs entirely on your own machine. There is no server, no account, and no company behind it. Your health data is never transmitted anywhere except between your own computer and your own provider.

Source code: github.com/sw181688-sys/personal-health-record-exporter — all of it, nothing hidden.

Who this is for

People who want a durable, portable copy of their own medical record rather than clicking through a patient portal one page at a time — and family caretakers who manage care for someone else and need the whole picture in one place.

If you are managing another person's care, use your provider's proxy or shared-access process so you sign in with your own credentials against their record. Don't use someone else's login.

What data this app requests, and why

The app requests read-only access to the categories below. Each is part of the US Core Data for Interoperability (USCDI) — the standard set of health data US patients have a legal right to access electronically.

What this app does not do

  • It cannot change anything in your medical record. The app only ever issues read requests. There is no code path in it that writes, updates, or deletes.
  • It does not send your data anywhere. Everything is written to a folder on your computer. No backend, no cloud storage, no analytics, no telemetry, no third-party services.
  • It does not see your password. You sign in on your provider's own login page in your own browser. The app receives only the authorization code handed back afterward.
  • It does not request refresh tokens by default, so access expires quickly and you re-authorize each time you run it.
  • It does not access anyone else's record. Access is limited to the patient who signed in.

Where your data goes

Into a folder you choose on your own computer:

record/
  raw/          the structured FHIR data, one file per category
  notes/        your clinical notes as plain text
  record.html   a readable, printable version of the whole record
  record.md     the same in Markdown
  .auth/        your access token (restricted to your user account)

Treat that folder the way you'd treat a printout of your chart. The .auth folder holds a credential that can read your record until it expires; the project's .gitignore keeps all of it out of version control.

Revoking access

You can disconnect this app from your record at any time — from your patient portal, not from this app. In MyChart®-based portals this is usually under Account Settings → Linked Apps and Devices (sometimes "Manage Access" or "Third-Party Apps"). Deleting the record/.auth folder on your computer also destroys the local copy of the token.

Security

  • Public OAuth 2.0 client using PKCE (RFC 7636) — no client secret, because a local script has nowhere safe to keep one.
  • The state parameter is verified on the callback to prevent request forgery.
  • The OAuth redirect is served over TLS on localhost only and is never exposed to the network.
  • Tokens are stored with filesystem permissions restricted to the account that ran the export — 0600 on macOS and Linux, and an equivalent ACL on Windows — and are never logged.
  • The access token is only ever sent to the health system it was issued for. URLs supplied by the server are checked against that origin first, so a token cannot be forwarded elsewhere.
